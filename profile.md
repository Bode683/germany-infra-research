## Derived flags (analysis, not facts)

1. **Experience is ~3 months, not ~3 years.** Earlier framing ("less than 3 years") overstated it. Consequence: the employment-first route (Route B) is very likely weak — a junior with 3 months SWE, no infra exposure and pre-B1 German competing for German jobs from Cameroon. Package 1 must confirm this quickly against official Chancenkarte / Blue Card / skilled-worker criteria rather than assume it. The master's route (A/C) becomes the probable main path.
2. **Grade is exactly at the common cut-off.** 3.0/4.0 with a 2.0 pass mark → German 2.5. Eligible for "2.5 or better" programmes, zero margin. Stricter or competitively ranked programmes are reach options.
3. **Credits are a strength.** ~257 credits, if ≈ ECTS, clears 210 and 240 thresholds → 3-semester master's programmes are open, which cuts cost and time. ~100 CS credits should satisfy most CS prerequisites; maths-heavy profile suits distributed systems / security.
4. **Funding is not a binding constraint for tuition-free programmes.** ~€24.6k at arrival plus €600/month continuing, plus a likely Verpflichtungserklärung from the brother. See `reports/01-feasibility.md` §4. TUM-type tuition (€4–6k/semester) is the exception.
5. **Zero infra experience makes student-job access harder.** Working-student infra roles usually expect Linux, networking, scripting and some cloud/containers. 11 months before arrival should produce demonstrable basics (home lab, GitHub projects, possibly one entry certification) — but this competes with German study for time. German is the gate; it takes priority.
6. **English proof is a medium-risk item.** An English-medium instruction letter is accepted by many programmes, not all. Some require IELTS/TOEFL regardless. Verify per programme; an IELTS (~€200–250) may be cheap insurance.
7. **Visa timing is THE bottleneck.** Yaoundé lists regular student-visa appointments at up to one year, waitlist only. Register now.
