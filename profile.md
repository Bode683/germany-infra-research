## Derived flags (analysis, not facts)

1. **Funding is not "covered" for a 2-year master's.** €18k vs. a 4-semester programme: living costs roughly €11–14k/year (blocked-account minimum is ~€11.9k/year — verify the current figure), plus semester fees (~€150–400 each), plus tuition where applicable (Baden-Württemberg ~€1,500/semester for non-EU; TUM charges non-EU tuition). €18k covers roughly 14–18 months, not 24. Without more savings, year 2 depends on student-job income — which violates the campaign's own decision constraint. 3-semester programmes and tuition-free states matter more than they looked.
2. **German B2 by Oct 2027 is a target, not a fact.** A2→B2 is typically several hundred guided study hours. Base case for planning: B1 certified. Treat B2 as the favourable scenario until a certificate exists. The job-market analysis must test both.
3. **Admission likely feasible on subject.** Maths + CS is a strong fit for CS/distributed-systems master's. Grade and credit totals are the unverified risk.
4. **Route B is serious, not a formality.** ~3 years SWE + native English + B1 German may support an employment-first move (e.g. Chancenkarte job-search permit, or a direct job offer under skilled-worker/Blue Card rules). This must be tested in Package 1 with current official criteria before committing ~€18k+ and 1.5–2 years to a degree.
